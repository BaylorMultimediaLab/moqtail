## Event contract changes

All events keep their existing fields unless stated. New fields are
additive so old bundles still parse.

### Switch identity (fixes C1)

- `SWITCH_SENT` gains `switch_seq` (per client session, starts at 1,
  monotonic). Every later record of the same switch carries the same
  `switch_seq`: `SWITCH_FLOOR` (pr1378), `SWITCH_OK`, `SWITCH_ERROR`,
  `SWITCH_FIRST_OBJECT`, `SWITCH_APPLIED`, `SWITCH_FIRST_FRAME`,
  `SWITCH_SUPERSEDED`.
- `SWITCH_SKIPPED {switch_seq, from, to, reason, pending_request_id}` is an
  attempt, not a switch: the player emits it _instead of_ a `SWITCH_SENT`
  (the previous switch has no alias yet), with a `switch_seq` of its own
  from the same counter. It never ends a switch; the analyzer counts it as
  `switches.skipped_not_sent` (diagnostic).
- `SWITCH_SUPERSEDED {switch_seq, by_switch_seq, playhead_ms}` is emitted
  when a newer switch lands before this one's seam was presented (the
  pending seam is overwritten). A switch therefore ends in exactly one of:
  `SWITCH_FIRST_FRAME`, `SWITCH_SUPERSEDED`, `SWITCH_ERROR`, or none (run
  ended first).
- The analyzer joins by `switch_seq` when present. For bundles without it
  the fallback join is: the last `SWITCH_SENT` with `ts <= ff.ts` whose
  `from` and `to` both match; every other unresolved switch to that target
  is superseded.

### Landing and seam (fixes M9, M10)

- `SWITCH_FIRST_OBJECT`: unchanged, the landing object (`group`, `object`,
  `landed_on_keyframe` moves here as well).
- `SWITCH_APPLIED` is emitted when the first target object passes the
  keyframe gate, i.e. the first object actually appended. Its
  `new_start_pts_ms`, `media_seam_gap_ms`, `seam_ahead_of_playhead_ms`
  refer to that object. New fields: `landing_object` (index of the landing
  object), `first_appended_object`, `discarded_before_keyframe` (objects
  dropped between them), `landed_on_keyframe` (of the landing object,
  kept for continuity).
- `SWITCH_FIRST_FRAME` fires only for a presented frame whose `mediaTime`
  lies in `[seam_pts, target_append_front]` at the time of presentation,
  where `target_append_front` is the end PTS of the last appended target
  object. New fields: `seam_behind_playhead` (bool,
  `seam_ahead_of_playhead_ms < 0` at landing), `source: 'rvfc' | 'raf'`.
- Late objects of an earlier subscription (amended 2026-10-05, every arm).
  The relay gives every subscription to a track the same alias (its
  relay track id), which draft-18 11.1 allows for the same track, so an
  object still in flight from an earlier subscription to the target track
  reaches the player under the new one. Each switch carries the lowest group
  its subscription can deliver: native, the source's last received group
  when the SWITCH was sent (the relay starts the target at or after what it
  sent of the source); pr1378, the Minimum Switching Group of the SWITCH
  (G_switch is at or above it), raised to G_switch once SWITCH_OK names it. A target object below it never lands the
  switch, and after the landing an object of the current track below it is
  never appended: both are `DROP_STALE{earlier-subscription}`. In the
  2026-10-05 preflight four pr1378 switches in three shaped runs landed one
  to three groups below the relay's start on such an object; one was
  appended over the buffered source group and the decoder failed
  (MEDIA_ERR_DECODE).

- Decode-order appends (amended 2026-10-05, every arm). MSE drops, silently,
  every frame after a decode-time discontinuity (a frame below the last one, or
  more than two frame durations above it) up to the next keyframe. Objects reach
  the player in arrival order, which is not decode order: a retransmission
  delays the tail of group g on its stream while g+1's stream delivers, and a
  catch-up or replay delivers older groups next to live ones. In arrival order
  this cost the rest of a group about 1 % of the time in the 2026-10-05
  preflight, on every arm, mostly at capacity steps and switch seams (the pr1378
  "seam holes" there were these). The player now appends through a scheduler
  (`lib/appendOrder.ts`): a frame continuing the last appended one is appended;
  a frame after a gap waits for it (while the gap makes progress, as a
  catch-up still delivering does; given up after 1 s without progress; a low buffer does
  not cut the wait short, since in preflight 3 that dropped a replay arriving in
  order 0.6 s behind live), then the gap is given up at the earliest held
  keyframe; a non-keyframe behind the append front is dropped. When a
  switch lands, the source's held frames are given up the same way first (what
  can be appended from the earliest held keyframe goes in before the target's
  init segment), then the order starts afresh at the target's keyframe. Each drop is
  `DROP_STALE{behind-append-front | abandoned-gap | track-changed}`, and SAMPLE
  carries `held_frames`.

### Samples and throughput (fixes M11, M12, M13)

- `SAMPLE` gains `buffer_contig_s` (end of the buffered range containing
  the playhead minus playhead; 0 if none) and `presented_track` (the track
  whose frames are being presented, resolved from the playhead against the
  list of (seam_pts, track) transitions). `buffer_s` keeps its meaning
  (last range end minus playhead) and is renamed in the docs as the total.
- `STALL_START.track` is the presented track.
- The library stamps `recvAt` (`performance.now()`) on every object at
  enqueue in the data-stream handler. `THROUGHPUT_SAMPLE` is computed from
  `recvAt` of the first and last object of the group (arrival spacing),
  gains `discarded_bytes` (bytes of objects of that group dropped by the
  player or the library) and `track` is the track the group belonged to.
- `DROP_STALE` gains reason `unrouted` (library-level discard of a stream
  whose alias has no route; `bytes` counted from the stream) and the
  library cancels such a stream with STOP_SENDING.

### Seeks and gaps (fixes M14)

- One gap-crossing policy. `SEEK.reason` is one of `startup`, `gap`
  (the playhead crossed a buffered hole or jumped to a later range; fields
  `from_ms`, `to_ms`, `gap_ms`, `deferred_ms`), `wedge` (no later range
  existed; recovery seek). The analyzer accepts the old reasons
  (`range-jump`, `unwedge`) as `gap`.
- `media_skipped_ms` per run = sum of `to_ms - from_ms` over `gap` seeks
  after the initial window.

### Decisions and probe timing (review 2026-10-04)

- `ABR_DECISION` stays emitted at the landing (the switch landed on its
  target) and keeps `decided_ts` (epoch ms of the decision) and
  `landed_after_ms`; it gains `switch_seq` (the seq of the switch it decided,
  the same page-wide counter as `SWITCH_SENT`). `ABR_SWITCH_PHANTOM` (the
  switch ended without landing) gains `switch_seq` and `decided_ts`. Two
  kinds of switch have neither record: one still pending at the run end, and
  one superseded before it landed (`SWITCH_SUPERSEDED` with `landed: false`:
  the player never calls the controller back for it, and the controller drops
  its pending record when a newer switch lands or it ages out; a switch superseded after it
  landed has its `ABR_DECISION`). The analyzer attributes both from the last
  `ABR_TICK` at or before the `SWITCH_SENT` whose chosen target is the switch
  target (source `tick`; `none` without such a tick), and records the source
  of every attribution (`switch_seq`, `decided_ts`, `legacy_ts`, `phantom`,
  `tick`).
  The analyzer never reads an `ABR_DECISION`'s `ts` as the decision time.
- `PROBE` gains `first_object_ms` and `last_object_ms` (epoch ms of the first
  and last probe object received); `dt_ms` keeps its meaning (request to the
  end of the read, including the request round trip and the idle wait).

## Controller `min` (W5)

Mechanism-neutral and client-type-neutral by construction. Inputs:
throughput SWMA over the last 5 group samples (arrival-spaced; amended
2026-10-06: the harmonic mean of the window, and a sample only from a complete
group, see below), the
contiguous buffer envelope, the instantaneous contiguous buffer, and the
switch history.

- Throughput samples (amended 2026-10-06, the freeze). A group whose last
  object can be told is sampled only when that object arrives; a group left
  incomplete for two group times closes without a sample. The window is
  aggregated by its harmonic mean (as MPC's predictor), not its arithmetic mean.
  Both against loss bursts: a group held behind a lost packet arrives in one
  burst when the retransmission releases it and its sample reads the receive
  buffer's rate. In the 2026-10-06 grid such samples read 20-338 Mbit/s on the
  1.5 Mbit/s step, and 14 of 69 up-switches in that phase rested on an estimate
  above 1.3 x the link (pr1378 time-shifted 6 of 13, pr1378 live-edge 0 of 21):
  an arm-dependent bias in the controller's input. On that grid's samples the
  two changes leave 2 of 69.
- `ThroughputRule`: up when `0.9 × SWMA ≥ bitrate(i+1)` and the dwell is
  satisfied; down to the highest rung with `bitrate ≤ 0.9 × SWMA`.
  Dwell: no up-switch until `upDwellGroups` (default 3) complete groups
  of the landed track have been received since the last landing (counted
  per track; a group delivered twice counts once).
- `EmergencyBufferRule` (STRONG): instantaneous contiguous buffer `== 0` →
  rung 0; the envelope (one GOP plus one tick, 1250 ms at 1 s GOPs) of the
  contiguous buffer `< 0.5 s` →
  highest rung with `bitrate ≤ 0.7 × SWMA` (amended 2026-10-04: on the
  instantaneous value the live-edge per-group sawtooth fired it every
  group, 45 switches vs 0 on the time-shifted client in 120 s; on the
  envelope 0 vs 0). The envelope starts at the first presented frame and
  the low branch is silent until it covers a whole window (amended
  2026-10-04, second review: pre-roll zeros or a window shorter than one
  group fired it once on the live-edge client only). The rule only ever
  lowers the rung.
- `SwitchHistoryRule` in veto mode, 60 s window. A drop is exempt from the
  history only while the playhead is at a presented seam (in media time,
  from the hole before the seam to `historyIgnoreGroupsAfterLanding`
  (default 2) GOPs after it), so the same seam hole is exempt on both
  client types (amended 2026-10-04: anchoring at the landing exempted it
  at the live edge only). Every other drop counts on both.
- Buffer signal: envelope of `buffer_contig_s` over one GOP plus one tick
  (1250 ms at 1 s GOPs; amended in the second review: it was a fixed
  1250 ms whatever the GOP).
- Everything else off: BOLA, Probe (and the probe track), InsufficientBuffer,
  BufferDrainRate, LatencyTrend, Abandon, L2A, LoLP, DroppedFrames.
- History and `ABR_DECISION` are recorded in `onTrackSwitched` only when
  the landed track equals the target (no phantom switches), for all arms.
- The probe denominator bug is fixed for `grid` regardless.
- `RUN_META.controller` records the arm and every effective constant
  (tick, dwell, window, envelope, safety factors).

Runner: `--controller min` is the default for every batch from now on;
`grid` and `baseline` remain selectable.

## Transport fairness (W3, W4)

- Every SUBSCRIBE and every SWITCH from the player carries
  `SubscriberPriority(0)` and `GroupOrder(Ascending)` on every branch; the
  relay's promoted/target subscription uses them (native keeps the original
  subscription's priority; pr1378 reads them from the SWITCH parameters).
- Publisher: one priority for all video variants (flag
  `--variant-priority`, default 128, recorded in PUBLISHER_CONFIG).
- Probe stream priority derived from `compute_stream_priority(255, 255,
Ascending, u64::MAX)` so it is below every video stream for any
  subscriber priority.
- Control and request streams (R3-D2, harness, every arm): the relay's control
  stream, every bidi request stream it accepts, the PUBLISH streams it opens
  toward subscribers and the SUBSCRIBE/TRACK_STATUS/FETCH streams it opens
  upstream run at `CONTROL_STREAM_PRIORITY` = i32::MAX. Data priorities are
  shifted one below it (saturating at i32::MIN), so control > every video
  band > probe. FETCH response streams use `compute_stream_priority` with the
  FETCH's SubscriberPriority (128 if absent), GroupOrder, the default publisher
  priority and the first group (was a literal 0).
- Every stream is opened with its priority in place before the first byte
  (R3-D6): on WebTransport the QUIC stream is opened directly, prioritised, and
  the WebTransport header written after (quinn queues a stream at the priority
  it has at its first write).
- Data streams (R3-D3, R3-D5): a stream the subscriber stopped is not reopened;
  the rest of that subgroup is dropped. A joining replay finishes the streams of
  groups whose publisher stream has closed (the newest group continues live).
- FIN'd streams stay resettable (amended 2026-10-05, every arm). A data stream
  is FIN'd when its group is complete, but behind a saturated link its bytes
  can sit in the relay's send buffer for seconds. Closing used to take the
  stream out of the connection's map and hold it until the subscriber had
  acknowledged everything, so a later reset of the subscription (UNSUBSCRIBE,
  too far behind; on pr1378 the switch seam) missed it and the stale bytes
  were delivered anyway. Each subscription now keeps its FIN'd, unacknowledged
  streams (`finishing_streams`), and its resets reach them; a reset after FIN
  abandons what is unsent. A reset also records the group from which it
  applies, so a close that runs after it (a group completing during the reset)
  resets instead of FIN'ing; a close takes a stream out of the connection's map
  only if it is still the same stream; and a reset ends the stream's
  acknowledgement wait (quinn does not wake it). On the native arms no switch
  path resets, so their delivery is unchanged. On pr1378, in the 2026-10-05
  preflight each time-shifted shaped run received ~1.9 MB of groups at or
  above the seams as unrouted streams (about 10 s of the 1.5 Mbit/s step),
  which is why its delivery rate read 0.7 of the link. The same review found
  that pr1378's switch teardown removed the replaced subscription through the
  path that cancels it before the seam-aware reset ran, so its open streams
  below the seam were reset as well (the "holes ahead of the playhead" the
  seam-aware reset of 2026-10-02 was meant to remove; that fix never took
  effect). The teardown now detaches the subscription without cancelling it:
  below the seam its streams FIN and deliver, at or above it they are reset,
  FIN'd ones included (`SWITCH_SOURCE_RESET.reset_open`, `reset_finished`).
  Known limit: a replaced switch target's catch-up FETCH is one stream over
  several groups; it stops being written at the new seam (R7-D1) but what it
  already queued above that seam is still delivered and dropped by the player
  as `post-seam`.
- Relay `--congestion-controller cubic|bbr`, default `cubic`; runner passes
  it explicitly and records it; a `--cc bbr` runner flag exists for the
  sensitivity batch.
- Relay keep-alive and idle timeout, alias timeouts and T_switch are passed
  explicitly by the runner with identical values on every branch.

## Scope of the comparison (decided 2026-10-06)

The paper compares switching mechanisms as they are specified, across the two
client types: as-shipped native SWITCH (`switch/native`, no correction flags)
and PR #1378 with the next-group floor (`switch/pr1378`). Improving a mechanism
is later work: the native forward-trigger arm (the two correction flags below)
stays in the code and the runner but is not part of the frozen grid.

## The native arms: what "as shipped" means (2026-10-04, review R5)

Both native arms run the relay built from `switch/native`. The as-shipped
arm passes neither correction flag; the fixed arm passes both
(`--forward-promotion-trigger`, `--native-status-before-subscribe`), and
the runner refuses a run whose `RELAY_CONFIG` disagrees with the arm.

As-shipped native keeps upstream moqtail's switch semantics on its default
path, checked line by line against `switch/native` before the rebuild: the
promotion-triggering object is never forwarded; the start is the old
subscription's last sent group + 1; the joining replay covers
[start, trigger] inclusive; the Next/Current statuses are set after the
SUBSCRIBE. What differs from upstream falls in two groups, and the paper
states both:

1. Transport and infrastructure, identical for every arm: stream priority
   set before a stream's first byte (so a new data stream is never queued
   at priority 0 for its first packet; pilot seam timings before the
   rebuild are therefore not comparable); control and request streams at
   the top priority; video priorities shifted by one below them; the probe
   and FETCH responses on the priority formula; replayed streams of
   complete groups finished; stream opens without the partition lock;
   UDP segmentation off; the congestion controller pinned (CUBIC); the
   event log off the forwarding path.
2. Harness corrections that change what as-shipped delivers only where
   upstream already failed or misbehaved:
   - a native SWITCH inherits SubscriberPriority and GroupOrder from the
     old subscription when it carries none (no effect in practice: the
     player sends both on every SWITCH);
   - the old subscription's last sent location advances only on a
     successful write (upstream advanced it on a failed one, moving the
     native start group);
   - a live object the joining replay already delivered is dropped before
     the write (upstream wrote it again and the serializer failed, or a
     second subgroup header went onto the open stream);
   - after the subscriber stops a data stream (STOP_SENDING), the rest of
     that subgroup is dropped rather than sent on a reopened stream with
     mis-numbered object ids.

Upstream properties deliberately kept on the as-shipped arm and reported:
the dropped trigger (object-1 landings and the discarded group), and the
fan-out-before-cache-add race that can leave the trigger out of the
promotion replay (R3-D9).

## Shaping (W2)

One topology for both queue models, host-side egress (relay → client):

```
root netem delay 20ms limit 10000          # propagation, never the bottleneck
  └ htb rate R ceil R                      # the bottleneck rate
      └ leaf: bfifo limit <queue_bytes>    # tail-drop, bytes (queue_pkts × 1500)
          or fq_codel limit 10240 target <t> interval 100ms
```

Client-side egress: `netem delay 20ms limit 10000` (no rate). Offloads
(`gso`, `tso`, `gro`, `tx-udp-segmentation`, `rx-udp-gro-forwarding`)
disabled on both veth ends in both namespaces before any qdisc is added;
`ethtool -k` recorded. Amended 2026-10-04: device offloads do not stop
QUIC's own UDP segmentation (quinn sends with `UDP_SEGMENT`; the kernel
keeps each batch as one skb through the qdisc and segments it after
dequeue). The relay therefore runs with `--udp-gso off` (quinn
`enable_segmentation_offload(false)`), recorded as `RELAY_CONFIG.udp_gso`,
and the runner reports `gso_at_qdisc` from the leaf's `maxpacket`. Capacity steps use `tc class change` (and
`tc qdisc change` for the leaf) so nothing queued is dropped. codel
`target` = max(5 ms, 1.5 × MTU time at the step's rate), recorded. The

## Preflight (W1 validator + guide)

`validate.py --preflight` on a 60 s run asserts, per arm and client type:

- keyframe landings = 100 % (every arm except as-shipped native, where the
  fraction is reported, not asserted);
- `landed_behind_playhead` = 0;
- every switch ends in exactly one terminal record;
- `DROP_STALE{unrouted}` bytes = 0 after the first switch has settled
  (reported if not);
- `RELAY_CONFIG.congestion_controller` matches the run identity;
- `qdisc_stats` present on every NET_CHANGE and the kernel tree recorded
  after the step (`qdisc_stats.tree`) has the expected levels, with the htb
  class at the step's rate (`qdisc_stats.htb_class`, amended 2026-10-05: the
  check read the step's commands, and a capacity step's `tc class change`
  names only htb, so every shaped run failed it);
- no switch's first object below the relay's start group for it
  (`pf-landing`, 2026-10-05);
- no media element error (`media-error`, every run, 2026-10-05: after one the
  decoder has stopped and nothing later is a measurement);
- on the unshaped profile: CONN_STATS loss rate < 0.1 %;
- `qdisc_stats.leaf.gso_at_qdisc` false on every NET_CHANGE of a rate-limited profile, and
  the leaf's `maxpacket` ≤ 1514 B (fail above 3000 B, report between);
  `RELAY_CONFIG.udp_gso` = `off`;
- delivery rate (amended 2026-10-04; replaces the probe check, since `min`
  runs without the probe): for each rate-limited step, over
  `[step + 10 s, next step)`, the median `THROUGHPUT_SAMPLE` rate of
  link-limited groups (bytes × 8 / 50 ms ≥ rate) lies in `[0.6, 1.15] × rate`.
  Out-of-band steps are attributed with the probe's pure transfer rate in
  the same window (objects 2..N over `last_object_ms − first_object_ms`;
  `dt_ms` on older bundles, noted in the detail): probe as low as video =
  transport; probe near the rate = client-side timing. In fresh-grid-v2, 14 of 30 runs fail after the
  restore to 6 Mbps with video and probe both at 1.2-1.7 Mbps on all arms
  and both client types: the relay's connection kept delivering the low
  step's rate (BBR then; CONN_STATS now records cwnd and rtt to show why).
  This is a transport effect, not the estimator; it must not recur under
  CUBIC before any batch counts. Preflight 2026-10-05 (CUBIC): every native
  and native forward-trigger run delivered at the link rate on all three
  steps, the restore to 6 Mbps included (5.65-5.72 Mbit/s); the pr1378
  time-shifted runs read 0.7 of the 1.5 Mbit/s step because of the unreset
  FIN'd streams above (Transport fairness).
